The surface of the ocean is familiar. Sunlit, restless, warm.
Beneath the waves, the realm of light begins to die.
At two hundred meters, red wavelengths are extinguished.
You cross into the twilight zone, where vision fades and shadow reigns.
One thousand meters down. Absolute darkness.
The sun is gone, never to return.
Four thousand meters. The abyssal plains stretch into eternity.
The pressure mounts to four hundred atmospheres.
And yet, deeper still, the seabed fractures into the abyss.
Six thousand meters. Welcome to the Hadal zone.
The tectonic scar of the Mariana Trench plunges into the mantle.
Ten thousand nine hundred and ninety-four meters.
Challenger Deep.
Here, under one thousand and eighty atmospheres of crushing pressure,
where Mount Everest would be submerged beneath two kilometers of water,
the silence of the Earth is complete.
In the deepest wound of our living planet, life still endures.
